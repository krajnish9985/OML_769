Only one evaluation metric has language rules, the agreement-error count, and those rules live in agreement.py. Everything else in evaluate.py is the same formula for all three languages.

Language-independent metrics, defined in evaluate.py

Metric	How it’s computed
%english	Share of letters in the language’s script. The ranges are in config.py → SCRIPT_RANGE: Devanagari for hin/mar, Tamil for tam. An output counts as English if less than 50% of its letters are in that script.
%looping	Share of repeated word 4-grams. Above 0.3 (REPETITIVE at the top of the file) counts as looping.
distinct2	Unique word pairs / all word pairs.
chrF++	Character overlap with the reference answer, using sacrebleu. Only Dolly has references.
judge_ppl	Perplexity under a stronger LM (--judge sarvamai/sarvam-1).
accept, tok/s	Copied from the SD stats.

Agreement errors (--stanza), rules in agreement.py → find_errors()

Stanza parses the text, and the rules check the parse:

Rule	hin	mar	tam
subj_verb: subject vs verb (gender, number, person)	✓	✓	✓ (finite verbs only)
adj_noun: inflecting adjective ending in -ा (बड़ा/बड़ी/बड़े) vs noun	✓	✓	✗
ne_object: with ने, the verb agrees with the object	✓	✗	✗
oblique: लड़का को → लड़के को	✓	✗	✗

The per-language exceptions are also in find_errors(). They’re there to cut false alarms:

hin/mar: Number isn’t checked for masculine nouns not ending in -ा (खिलाड़ी is the same in singular and plural).
Hindi: skips subjects marked with ने or को. The NON_INFLECTING_A list covers words like राजा and पिता that don’t become oblique.
Tamil: no Number check for neuter subjects, since a neuter plural with -கிறது is normal.
All languages: pronouns are checked for gender only, and the checks skip proper nouns, words in lists, and numbers.

Module A (S2) uses the same find_errors() during decoding. Its extra surface rules (broken matra, script mixing, repetition) are in modules/rules.py.

As we found earlier, the agreement count is trustworthy for Hindi only. For mar/tam the parser is weak, so rely on MultiBLiMP plus hand checks there.
